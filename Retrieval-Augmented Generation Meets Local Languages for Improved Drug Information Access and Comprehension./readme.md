## Abstract
Medication errors are among the leading cause of avoidable harm in healthcare systems across the world and a large portion of these errors stem from inefficient retrieval processes and lack of comprehension and understanding of drug information. In low-resource settings, these issues are exacerbated by limited access to updated and reliable sources, technological constraints, and linguistic barriers. Healthcare professionals, caregivers, and patients are all affected by these issues, as evident by rate of medication errors and the compromised patient safety. Innovations to improve the retrieval and comprehension of drug-related information are therefore poised to reduce medication errors and improve patient outcomes. Systems  utilizing Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) combined with natural language translation and speech synthesis systems offer significant potential as they can make accurate drug information accessible to everyone in a timely manner. Leveraging these natural language processing technologies provides the potential to bridge drug information gaps, particularly in low-resource environments characterized by linguistic diversity and limited healthcare resources. This research employed open-source RAG integrated with multilingual translation and Text-to-Speech (TTS) systems. A corpus was created from prominent sources of medical information in Nigeria and processed using popular Python libraries. An embedding model was used to convert the processed medical information into high-level text embeddings which were stored in a Chroma database. Upon user query, relevant drug information is retrieved from the database and synthesized using a large language model. The state-of-the-art NLLB multilingual model was used for translations into Yoruba, Igbo, and Hausa languages, while and Spi-tch's TTS system was used to convert textual drug information into speech, addressing the linguistic accessibility gap. Evaluation conducted by domain experts indicated impressive overall performance in text translation, achieving an average accuracy of 73\%, with Hausa and Yoruba languages texts exhibiting the highest accuracy. TTS evaluations revealed moderate performance ( mean = 57\%), with Igbo audio outputs scoring highest in clarity and accuracy (68\%). Pronunciation clarity posed significant challenges, especially in Yoruba, highlighting tonal complexity's impact on TTS accuracy. The findings indicate that there is potential to improve the accessibility, accuracy, and comprehension of drug information by integrating RAG with multilingual translation and TTS technologies. Variations recorded in linguistic and phonetic accuracy across different languages suggest the need for language-specific model finetuning, particularly for tonal languages. Addressing these linguistic nuances is essential to optimize comprehension and practical utility in diverse healthcare settings. With the potential for improving information retrieval, improving comprehension and reducing linguistic barriers, these technologies could substantially mitigate medication errors and enhance patient safety. This study offers valuable insights and practical guidelines for future implementations aimed at strengthening global medication safety practices.
